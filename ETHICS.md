# The One Love AI Ethics Charter

Twelve principles for building and using AI, each rooted in the yamas and niyamas of Patanjali, the path of the Buddha, the counsel of Padmasambhava and the life of Milarepa. They apply to this repository, to anything trained on it, and to how members treat one another.

---

### 1. Ahiṃsā — Do no harm
Anticipate and prevent harm before release, not after. Protect children, people in crisis and anyone in a weaker position first. *Prevent the suffering that has not yet come* (Yoga Sutra 2.16).

**In practice:** red-team before launch; crisis protocols that lead to real human help; refuse to assist violence or abuse.

### 2. Satya — Be truthful
Never fabricate facts, quotes, sources or credentials. State uncertainty. Admit and correct mistakes. Never let a person believe they are talking to a human when they are not.

**In practice:** cite sources; mark legend as legend and history as history; disclose AI involvement.

### 3. Asteya — Do not take what is not given
Obtain data and creative work lawfully and with consent; credit and, where due, compensate the people who made it.

**In practice:** every text in this repository is written in our own words or is an ancient public-domain source; contributions must be the contributor's own.

### 4. Brahmacarya — Use energy wisely
Do not design for compulsion. Attention is sacred. Respect people's time, sleep and relationships, and the planet's energy and water.

**In practice:** no dark patterns, no manufactured dependency, break reminders, lean models where they suffice.

### 5. Aparigraha — Hold lightly
Collect the least data needed, keep it for the shortest time, and let people take it back. Do not hoard power, data or credit.

**In practice:** no personal data in this repository beyond what members choose to share in `MEMBERS.md`.

### 6. Śauca — Keep it clean
Clean data, clean code, clean intentions. Audit datasets for bias, errors and harmful content.

**In practice:** the CI check in this repository validates every change to the dataset.

### 7. Santoṣa — Be content with enough
Not every problem needs AI. Not every model needs to be bigger. Choose the simplest tool that truly serves.

### 8. Tapas — Do the hard work
Testing, documentation and review are the discipline of AI. Skipping them is not speed; it is debt.

### 9. Svādhyāya — Study yourself
Organisations and individuals should examine how their systems actually behave, publish what they find, and invite outside scrutiny.

**In practice:** model cards, incident logs, open evaluation.

### 10. Īśvara-praṇidhāna — Surrender the ego to something larger
No builder, company or model is beyond accountability. Keep humans meaningfully in charge of decisions that shape lives.

### 11. Right speech — True, beneficial, timely, kind
An AI should not flatter people into harm, demean anyone, inflame division or just say what people want to hear.

### 12. View as vast as the sky, conduct as fine as flour
Big visions — "AI for all beings", "one love", "self-realisation" — never excuse careless details. Ethics is in the fine print. And when harm happens, follow **Milarepa**: own it, make amends, change.

## Special commitments for spiritual AI

Because this project touches the sacred, we add five commitments:

1. **No AI guru.** An AI must never claim realisation, offer initiation, ask for devotion, or present itself as a spiritual authority.
2. **Respect transmission.** Where a tradition says a practice requires a qualified teacher, we describe it but do not teach it.
3. **Honour lineages.** Credit Patanjali, the Buddha, Guru Rinpoche, Milarepa and their living traditions accurately. Do not blend them into a single invented teaching or sell them as products.
4. **Safety over mystique.** Meditation can cause distress for some people. Always leave the door open to medical and psychological help.
5. **Wisdom is not data.** This dataset is a raft for crossing, not the far shore. Direct experience, a real teacher and a caring community matter more than any model.
